Macro Mechanics & Transmission Channels

How policy, inflation, and currency moves actually travel through markets — not just what moved.

01
Rates

Yield-Curve Dynamics & Term Premium

Long-end yields move for two separable reasons: the market's expectation of the future policy path, and the term premium — the extra compensation investors demand for holding duration through an uncertain path, which rises independently when sovereign issuance is heavy or fiscal credibility is in question.

Right now: Bund (~3.35%), gilt (~5.16%) and JGB (3.00%) yields are all elevated even where central banks are only debating a hold-versus-hike, not accelerating cuts — a signal that term premium, not just rate-path expectations, is doing a lot of the work.

02
Inflation

PPI-to-CPI Passthrough & the CPI/PCE Gap

Producer-price increases in energy, freight, and industrial inputs typically pass through to core consumer goods prices with a 2–3 month lag. CPI and PCE can also diverge because PCE uses a broader, chain-weighted basket that reweights toward substitutes faster than CPI's fixed-basket method.

Right now: July core CPI printed 2.5% y/y while core PCE — the Fed's preferred gauge — ran hotter at 3.3% y/y, which is why the Fed can sound hawkish even when the headline CPI print looks tame.

03
FX

Sovereign Balances & Currency Resilience

A current-account surplus nation with a large net foreign-asset position (like Japan) has structural currency support. A persistent deficit nation needs continuous foreign capital inflows, which makes its currency and bond yields more sensitive to a confidence shock.

Right now: USD/JPY has rallied toward ~156 on a narrowing rate differential and intervention speculation, while the UK's gilt-market wobble ahead of the Autumn Budget is a textbook deficit-country confidence test.

§2

Micro Fundamentals & Equity Mechanics

Reading through headline earnings and commodity prices to the underlying physical and accounting mechanics.

04
Equity

Earnings Quality & CapEx Conversion

Capital spent today doesn't hit the income statement as an expense immediately — it's capitalized and depreciated over years. The analyst discipline is tracking the capex-to-depreciation ratio and free-cash-flow conversion over several quarters, not just current-quarter revenue growth.

Right now: with hyperscaler 2026 capex guided to roughly $690–800B, the real test of the AI investment thesis is operating-margin trajectory over the next 4–8 quarters — not this quarter's topline growth.

05
Commodities

Backwardation vs. Contango

Backwardation — spot or near-month price above forward months — signals immediate physical scarcity: buyers pay a premium for metal or oil delivered now as visible warehouse inventories draw down. Contango signals ample supply or high storage costs, which incentivizes buyers to defer purchases.

Right now: copper's steep backwardation reflects a genuine mix of supply loss (Chile output down 9.4% y/y, a Congo export restriction) and tariff-driven strategic stockpiling in US warehouses — part signal, part positioning.

§3

Analyst Learning Notes

Standalone mechanics worth internalizing — generalizable beyond this month's headlines.

✎ Learning Note

Reading a Futures Curve Shape

Always ask two questions before trading a curve shape: is inventory actually falling (check LME/COMEX warehouse data), and is demand for immediate delivery genuinely rising, or is a large buyer front-loading purchases for reasons unrelated to consumption? Backwardation from the first cause is a demand signal; from the second, it's a positioning artifact.

✎ Learning Note

Term Premium Is Not the Same as Rate Expectations

A 10-year yield can rise even while a central bank holds rates steady, if investors demand more compensation for duration risk — from heavier debt issuance, fiscal concerns, or inflation-persistence fears. Decompose a yield move before reading it as a policy forecast.

✎ Learning Note

Why CPI and PCE Can Diverge

CPI uses a fixed consumer-spending basket updated infrequently; PCE is broader, chain-weighted, and reweights toward cheaper substitutes faster. A gap this wide usually reflects category-weight differences — shelter and imputed healthcare costs typically matter most.

✎ Learning Note

Current Account Balances Predict Currency Fragility

A current-account surplus nation (Japan) funds itself; a deficit nation (the UK) borrows continuously from abroad to do so. That's why a UK fiscal event moves gilts and GBP together, while a similar shock in a surplus economy is more easily absorbed.

✎ Learning Note

Capex Today, Depreciation Tomorrow

A capex boom flatters revenue and sentiment immediately but only shows its true return once the depreciation schedule catches up, typically 2–4 years out. Track ROIC and free-cash-flow conversion now so a margin disappointment later isn't a surprise.
